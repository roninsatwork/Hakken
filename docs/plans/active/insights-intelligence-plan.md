# Insights: the intelligence an agency is paid for

Status: proposed, 12 October 2026. Nothing here is built. Drawn on the canvas
"Hakken Insights — intelligence" for approval before any of it is built.

## Why

Anthony, 12 October 2026, after the Executive summary only spoke in years:

- "An agency would report on month on month gains or losses and quarter on
  quarter losses too. These could correlate to competitors found better or
  Google core updates."
- "We need to add more intelligence in the Insights section based on the vast
  amount of data we have and keep."
- His example: "On Ronins we have two pages, brand design agency and branding
  identity agency. I suspect they are keyword cannibalising, but I don't know
  which page drives conversions, what keywords bring those to it, which
  keywords should bring more traffic if we are higher in the rankings. These
  are the things I want Hakken to research and find, and this is what you
  would pay an agency for."
- "There may be many pages competing; it may not be a pair." "Keyword
  cannibalisation sits in Findings, then summaries on the Executive summary."
- On search words to enquiries: estimates agreed (1); the enquiry form's own
  question agreed (2); "we get more detail in DataForSEO around this" (3).
- "What is enquiries — is this conversions or a group of different
  conversions?" A group: the Analytics events ticked as enquiries, added up.
  Showing each kind on its own agreed ("agree").
- "I want a specific keyword cannibalisation section. This should be under
  Findings."
- On the canvas, 12 October: "we don't split enquiries down by page
  classification at all — I think we need this, it's important"; "Ronins'
  data is skewed by articles"; "overall we need to remember by
  classifications — product, service, information etc. You would expect an
  agency you pay to go into this detail"; "show which assistant or Google
  brought those enquiries … a detail click-through page"; on AI search, "a
  graph over time, say the last year — are we growing or not"; on What moved,
  "there are good graphs and charts to be had here"; on Findings, "too
  complete and text heavy … separate out the sections into the left hand
  nav"; and "why so many drop downs … why two different 90 days".

The research behind this plan (read-only, 12 October) found Hakken already
keeps most of what is needed and uses little of it in Insights: Search
Console's search-by-page pairs and its "Pages competing" list (1,004 searches
split across 78 Ronins pages), Analytics' enquiries by landing page and by
landing page and channel, five AI assistants' answers kept for good, and
Google's updates. What is missing is the reading: joining them and saying why.

## What Hakken will find

### Throughout: by kind of page, and one period choice

- Every figure in Insights is split by the company's own page
  classifications (Ronins: Service, Brand, Content hub, Journal, Insights,
  Case studies, Authors, Product, Legal…) and added up by their type —
  service, information, case study, product. A whole-website figure mixes
  pages that sell with pages that inform. Ronins, last 12 months: service
  pages bring 38% of visits and 52% of enquiries (20 visits for each);
  articles (Content hub, Journal, Insights) 28% of visits and 11% of
  enquiries (66 visits for each); Brand (home, about) 18% and 28%.
- Pages since merged or removed are counted by what they were (a merged
  service page as a service page), or a year's comparison loses them:
  /blockchain-developers/ brought 12 enquiries the year before.
- The Insights header holds the only website and period choice; no page adds
  its own.

### 1. Months and quarters, and why each moved

- A **Month / Quarter / Year** choice on the Executive summary and across
  Insights. Each period compared two ways: with the period just before (this
  month against last) and with the same period a year ago. The page opens on
  Month.
- Every gain or loss over the chosen period comes with **why**: the most
  likely cause, its evidence and what was ruled out —
  - rivals: who gained the searches and AI mentions you lost that month;
  - Google: an update in the same weeks, from the Google updates Hakken keeps;
  - your own changes: from the change log (pages rewritten, merged, removed);
  - measurement: a tracking fault that makes the figure untrue.
- The Head of Strategy leads with the month when it is the sharper change, and
  every figure says its period (the agents' briefs, 12 October).
- "What moved" is drawn, not only written: enquiries by kind of page and by
  source against the period before, and how far the articles skew the
  website's figures, above the causes weighed.
- Data: monthly totals kept for two years (visits, people, enquiries, by
  channel), one Analytics ask a run; Search Console's months from its days.
  Today Hakken keeps 12 months of days, which cannot compare a quarter with
  the same quarter a year ago.

### 2. What brings enquiries — a new Insights screen

Rethought on 12 October into sections, kinds of page first: by kind of page
(enquiries against a year before, share of visits against share of
enquiries, month by month, and each classification), by where people came
from (Google, each AI assistant by name, typed in, other websites — each row
opening that source's own page: its enquiries over time and the pages and
kinds of page it sends people to), by kind of enquiry, then the searches and
pages. Ronins, 12 months: Google 93 enquiries (151 the year before), typed in
41 (43), ChatGPT 15 (9), Bing 7 (19), Gemini 3 (0). Enquiries fell by 72:
Brand pages lost 31, articles 20, service pages 14, while service pages drew
40% more visits.

- **Searches**: each search's enquiries, estimated by sharing a page's Google
  enquiries across its searches by their share of its clicks — said as
  "about", with how sure. Month by month, against a year ago. Searches that
  bring clicks but no enquiries.
- **Pages**: every page by enquiries, split by where people came from (Google,
  AI assistants, direct, other websites), month by month.
- **Each kind of enquiry** (agreed 12 October): "enquiries" is a group — the
  Analytics events ticked as enquiries on the website's Analytics connection,
  added up. The screen shows each kind on its own, month by month and against
  a year ago: forms, live chats, phone number clicks, email address clicks,
  and an event the website records but doesn't count. A kind ticked but never
  recorded is a Measurement finding of its own. Ronins: all 169 enquiries in
  12 months were forms (241 the year before); live chats, phone clicks and
  email clicks are ticked but none was recorded in two years; an event called
  "contact" happened 67 times and isn't counted.
- **Pages read on the way to enquiring**: a new Analytics ask (a funnel of
  "viewed this page, then enquired") for the money pages and the top guides.
- Google-only figures use Analytics' landing page by channel, not every
  channel, which Opportunities and Page reviews use today.
- The enquiry form's own question, "What did you search for to find us?", is
  offered to the client as the only way to know the words (their website, not
  Hakken's).

### 3. Keyword cannibalisation — its own section under Findings

- A **Keyword cannibalisation** page in the Insights menu, under Findings,
  after All findings (Anthony, 12 October). It lists every group of
  competing pages, biggest first: its searches split, times shown, Google
  clicks, enquiries against a year before, the page that should own the
  searches, and how sure. A chart sets the groups' times shown beside their
  clicks. Each row opens the group's finding.
- Hakken finds **groups** of competing pages (pages linked by the searches
  they share), not only pairs, from Search Console's competing list. Searches
  for the website's own name are left out, and the home page's enquiries are
  never counted in a group: it brings them for everything.
- Ronins, last 90 days: eight groups, 25 pages, 1,377 searches split, shown
  275,377 times for 52 clicks — app development (5 pages, 640 searches,
  114,654 times shown, 12 clicks), web design (4, 249, 73,755, 7), web design
  in Surrey (4, 153, 41,300, 7), AI (3, 175, 27,216, 20, and 37 enquiries in
  12 months), UX design, online shops, digital marketing, and branding (4, 61,
  1,640, 1).
- Each group is a **finding** in Findings, with its detail screen answering:
  the searches they share and how Google splits the clicks; which page brings
  the enquiries; which searches bring people to each page; which searches
  would bring more with a higher place (extra clicks at a top-three place,
  times the page's enquiry rate); and one owner page for each search, with a
  decision for every other page — merge it into the owner with a redirect,
  make it clearly different, or keep it and link to the owner — and what it
  expects to gain, checked on the day it names.
- The Executive summary sums them up ("Eight groups of your pages are
  splitting Google's searches"), linking to Keyword cannibalisation.
- Each finding weighs its searches by how often people search them, not only
  by how often Google showed the website (Anthony, 12 October, "yes" to
  DataForSEO volumes informing the guidance): when Hakken writes a keyword
  cannibalisation finding, it buys the monthly search volume of the group's
  searches from DataForSEO, shows it beside each search, and works out what a
  higher place would bring from it. A page on page two is shown for a few of
  the searches made, so times shown understates demand. Today Hakken holds a
  volume for 533 of the 9,040 searches Google shows Ronins for (the tracked
  ones); of the branding searches only "brand agency surrey" (110 a month).
- Google switching which page it shows for a search is counted as a sign.

### 4. Page decisions — the Page plan taken further

- A verdict for **every** page, not only money pages and guides: keep,
  improve, merge into another, redirect, remove, or create a missing page.
- From visits, enquiries, Google clicks, the links pointing at the page
  (kept already, never used), its overlap with other pages (3), the site
  audit's duplicate, thin and orphan flags, indexing, and pages nobody visits.
- A page with links pointing at it is never removed without a redirect.
- "Create" from the content gap and the buyers' questions (defined, never used).

### 5. AI search over time — a new Insights screen

- Per assistant (ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini, Google AI Mode), month
  by month against each rival: named, recommended, cited. The data is kept
  (`siteListAiDays`, `aiCitations`); cited pages need a monthly roll-up.
- Visits and enquiries each assistant sent, month by month (today 60 days of
  days only).
- From DataForSEO, beyond what is bought today: mentions in ChatGPT as well as
  Google's AI (LLM Mentions, `platform: "chat_gpt"`), and AI search volume
  (how often a topic is asked of assistants). Each priced per client per month
  before it is bought.
- The history starts on 23 September 2026; the screen says so until a year is
  held.
- "Are you growing in AI?" first: visits and enquiries the assistants sent,
  month by month over the last year, by assistant against the year before,
  each 90 days, and by kind of page. Ronins: 258 visits in 12 months (159 the
  year before) and 19 enquiries (11) — ChatGPT 228 visits (107) — but 25 in
  the last 90 days, against 62 in the 90 before and 76 a year ago.

### 6. Findings in sections

- Findings is split into sections in the Insights menu: Overview, Keyword
  cannibalisation, Enquiries and pages, Search and content, AI answers,
  Rivals, links and reviews, Technical and measurement, Track record. Each
  section lists its own findings.
- The overview is short: findings each week by section, when Hakken checks
  them, and each section's biggest finding in one line. Ronins: 13 findings
  the week of 5 October and 11 the week of 12 October; 24 checks due from 10
  November to 10 January.

- The counting check (`countingCheck`): Analytics' visits from Google set
  against Google's own clicks, day by day — a sudden step (eight weeks either
  side, a move of a quarter or more) and the share now against the same 90
  days a year before.

- No counting fault on 3 August — the first reading compared Analytics'
  visits from every source with Google's clicks. Like with like, Analytics
  sees 0.49 of Google's clicks now against 0.62 a year ago: a slow drift, no
  step.
- What fell in August was visits from elsewhere: AI assistants (22 in July, 1
  in August, 6 in September), Bing and the other search engines, typed in.
- Service pages, June to September against a year before: Google's clicks up

  May core updates.

## Faults found on the way

- The "Recommended by AI" league counts any mention, not recommendations; the
  cited league's year-before value is never written.
- A search's position and its ranking page can come from two different pages
  on the same day (`siteRankings.ts`).
- The Editor let a paragraph set one rival's November against its October as a
  year apart: months compared must be the same month.
- Ronins ticks live chats, phone number clicks and email address clicks as
  enquiries, but Analytics hasn't recorded one in two years: the website may
  not be sending them. An event named "contact" (67 times in 12 months) isn't
  ticked. Hakken should say both by itself, as a Measurement finding.
- The first drawing's "What brings enquiries" said 135 enquiries in 12 months;
  the stored total is 169 (241 the year before). The branding example said 12
  months of Search Console, which Hakken doesn't hold yet. Both corrected on 12
  October.

### Built so far (12 October)

- **Phase 8** and the core of **phase 1** (commit c1661f2f): one period choice
  in the Insights header — a month, a calendar quarter or the last 12 months —
  each against the period before and a year ago; the Executive summary's
  figures follow it; "Why your enquiries fell" with the split and the six
  tests; the Insights: Analyst agent.
- **Phase 1's "What moved"**: enquiries by kind of page and by where people
  came from against the period compared, and how far the articles skew the
  figures (Ronins, September 2026: articles 23% of visits, 10% of enquiries),
  above the six tests.
- **Phase 2**: What brings enquiries under Growth, and a page for each source.
  The history now also keeps each landing page by source and each event a
  month, the pages read in visits that enquired and Search Console's searches
  by page over the last 90 days. Searches are estimated over those 90 days
  only: a search's history month by month would keep every search a month,
  which the keep-less-history plan stopped. A page's estimate is shared over
  all of its Google clicks, the private searches included, and says how sure
  it is.
- **Phase 3**: Keyword cannibalisation under Findings, a page for each group,
  the biggest five written up as findings each week (built from the group's
  own figures), the Executive summary's summing up, and the groups' searches'
  monthly volumes bought from DataForSEO (Google Ads' figures, one request a
  week at most). A group is pages that split at least three searches between
  them (each taking a tenth of the showings or more); a group bigger than six
  is split on its stronger links and no page is dropped. The owner is the page
  bringing the most enquiries; a page is merged into it only when it brings no
  enquiries and a third or more of its showings are the group's searches
  (Ronins' /brand-design-agency/: 39%); an article is kept and links to the
  owner; any other page is made clearly different. What a top-three place
  would bring uses the website's own click rate at the top three and its
  enquiry rate (a page's own from 100 visits), each close variant counted
  once. Ronins, 13 July to 10 October: six groups, 29 pages, 221,590 times
  shown for 50 clicks.
- **Phase 4**: the Page plan is now Page decisions: every page of the
  website's page list gets a verdict each week — improve, merge, redirect,
  remove or keep — kept with the week, beside the Page plan's own pages most
  at stake and the pages to create. A broken page is redirected when anything
  links to it, else taken out; Keyword cannibalisation's merges and the Page
  plan's actions come next; a page nobody viewed in a year, that Google didn't
  show in 90 days, is removed — redirected when another website links to it,
  and never a page a website needs (service, company, legal, product) or a
  list's further pages; then the site audit's duplicate, thin (too little
  text: its text-to-code ratio flags nearly every page, so it isn't read),
  not-indexable and orphan flags. Pages to create are searches two rivals win
  that the website has no page for, searched 50 times a month or more, each
  close variant once, then buyers' questions it doesn't rank for. Hakken has
  no indexing state for every page, so Google not showing a page in 90 days
  stands in for it. Ronins: 181 pages — 28 to improve, 3 to merge, 5 to
  remove, 125 to keep — and 20 pages to create.
- **Phase 5**: AI search under Growth. "Are you growing in AI?" first: each
  assistant's visits and enquiries month by month from the 16 months held,
  the newest three months against the three before and the same three a year

  answers to the company's questions naming and recommending it against each
  rival (kept from 23 September 2026, said on the screen), the pages each
  cites month by month, and — where bought — Google's AI mentions and how

  assistants against 61 the quarter before and 81 a year before; ChatGPT 23
  against 46. Every answer naming Ronins also recommends it: its questions
