**In plain words.** Every other section of Hakken is a report: it answers a
question someone chose in advance. Insights is the analyst. Each week it reads
every source for every owned website together, with the website's rivals,
notices what none of them shows alone, tests it against the data, and writes
up the few findings that matter, each ending in what to do. Around those
findings sit the things a client pays an agency for: an executive summary, a
monthly report, a quarterly strategy, a board report, leaderboards against
rivals, and the SEO and CRO work, all written by Hakken with no person in the
loop.

**The test that proved it.** On 2026-10-11 the method was run by hand on
ronins.co.uk's development data (read only). It found what no screen shows:
Google sent the same number of visitors as the same weeks last year (1,371
against 1,387) but they made 21 enquiries instead of 59, while direct visitors
held, so the enquiry form works and the change is in who Google sends; ronins
is first on Google for "ai agency" and takes about 80% of the five agencies'
visitors from AI searches, yet ChatGPT and Google's AI Mode named it in none of
their answers to its buyers' questions and both Business Profile listings file
it as a web designer; AI research tools searched "ronins.ai" 6,904 times in 90
days and found no company facts; no Google review since August 2024. Every
figure on the canvas is that real data unless §11 says otherwise. The
Analytics figures here counted every country. Measured again UK with UK on
2026-10-11 (I33), the story holds: over the 12 months to 10 October, UK visits
rose 6% (4,494 against 4,248) while enquiries fell 30% (169 against 241),
search visits rose 2%, and visitors from AI assistants enquired at 7.3%
against search's 3.1%. The same weeks against last year are read in phase 1
(I43), and the enquiry count itself is checked before any customer sees a
finding about it (I42).

## 2. How the team works

1. **One reading, not a copy.** Every source is read where it already is, a
   website at a time, in the team's background run; nothing is copied into a
   new table (§8). The joins exist already: pages carry one number in Search
   Console and Analytics (`holdPageRefs.ts`), searches match DataForSEO
   keywords (`normaliseKeyword`), and rivals sit beside the website in every
   DataForSEO table.
2. **Each specialist sweeps its own discipline.** Code first, with no AI:
   every change, trend, gap and thing that moves together in its slice,
   checked against its discipline's own checklist (§6).
3. **Each specialist thinks and tests.** The AI is given only the candidates
   worth a look, with a short digest of the figures. It explains them and tests
   each explanation against the data, dropping what doesn't hold, the way the
   ronins test ruled out a broken form because direct visitors still enquired.
4. **The Head of Strategy connects them.** Findings from different
   specialists that share a cause become one story across sources. Example:
   the chatbot page lost its enquiries (Conversion) because buyers now search
   for AI agents (Search), and ChatGPT names a rival for it (AI visibility).
   The Head of Strategy ranks everything by what is at stake and keeps one
   voice (I14).
5. **The Editor checks** every figure, comparison and claim (I21).
6. **Written and saved.** Each finding with its evidence, how sure Hakken is,
   what it is worth, what to do (and, for what lives off the website, the
   drafted fix, I23) and how we will know. The Report Writer turns them into
   the week's brief and the reports.
7. **Keep score.** On the date each finding names, Hakken checks whether it
   was right, beside the change log (I25), and puts the result on the Track
   record, per specialist. Across every website this becomes the record of
   which fixes work, which nobody else has.

## 7. Search Console: what the test found, and the fixes

Found on 2026-10-11 while checking the canvas figures:

1. **Rows without a country are the home country, not every country.** Every
   Search Console ask for a website's main scope is filtered to its home
   country (`searchConsoleSync.ts`, `countryFilter`), by the home-countries
   plan; only the country list is kept whole. For ronins.co.uk in the last 90
   days: 1,070 UK clicks, 2,062 worldwide. The schema comments still say
   "missing means all countries" (`searchConsoleSchema.ts`).
2. **Inside the home country, Google ties only about half the clicks to a
   page.** On all 59 days the lists cover, the page list's clicks equal the
   search-and-page pairs' clicks exactly (356 of 676 UK clicks over 58 days),
   while the daily totals and the device list agree with each other. With the
   country filter on, Google's page answer leaves out the searches it keeps
   private. Hakken adds up what it is given correctly; the gap is Google's. It
   can be confirmed in Search Console: Performance, Country = United Kingdom,
   22 September 2026: the chart says 17 clicks, the Pages table adds up to 11.
3. **Like with like.** When this was found, Analytics counted every country,
   so it was compared with Search Console's worldwide clicks: Analytics saw
   about two-thirds of them (1,371 visits against 2,062 clicks in 90 days),
   mostly visitors who decline cookies. **Changed 2026-10-11:** the Google
   Analytics work now filters every Analytics ask to the home countries, so
   the two are compared home country with home country (I33).

## 7b. Microsoft Clarity — how it connects (I24)

From Microsoft's documentation, checked 2026-10-11
(https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/clarity/clarity-data-export-api):

- **On the website:** Clarity's tracking code installed once (in the page
  header, through Google Tag Manager, or a WordPress plugin). For visitors
  from the UK, the EEA and Switzerland, Clarity has required a consent signal
  since 31 October 2025: Consent Mode switched on in Clarity's settings, and
  the site's cookie banner passing each visitor's choice to Clarity. Without
  it, Clarity's figures for those visitors are cut back.
- **The connection:** a Clarity project admin opens Settings → Data Export →
  Generate new API token and pastes the token into the website's Clarity
  connection in Hakken, as Google Analytics is connected. Kept with the
  connector tokens, never in the browser; tested with one ask when pasted.
- **What Hakken can ask:** the dashboard's figures (scroll depth, engagement
  time, traffic, dead clicks, rage clicks, quick backs, excessive scroll,
  script and error clicks), split by up to three of URL, device, channel,
  source and the like. **Only the last 1 to 3 days**, so Hakken asks every
  day and keeps the history itself. **At most 10 asks per project a day**, and
  1,000 rows an answer, no paging: Hakken uses one or two asks a day (by page,
  and by page and device), keeping each money page's figures packed, as the
  other day lists are.
- **What it can't do:** heatmap pictures and session recordings are not in
  the export; a page review links to Clarity's own heatmap for that page.
- **Cost:** Clarity is free; one or two asks a day.
