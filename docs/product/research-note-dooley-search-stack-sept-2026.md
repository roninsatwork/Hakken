# Hakken — research note: James Dooley's "Modern Search Optimisation Stack"

**20 September 2026: the product is now named Hakken (発見, discovery). References below to "the product" or "the platform" mean Hakken; the current plan is app-vision-v2.md.**


**Source:** James Dooley (@james_dooley), X, 18 September 2026. UK lead-gen entrepreneur; founder of PromoSEO and FatRank; builds digital real estate on a guaranteed-ROI model.
**Filed:** 18 September 2026. Fourth document in the research set.

---

## What he says

Search optimisation is no longer about ten blue links. Discovery, research and purchase decisions have fragmented across search engines, social platforms, AI-generated answers, recommendation systems and, increasingly, autonomous agents. A customer might find a company on TikTok, research it on Google, ask ChatGPT to compare it, check Reddit, and ask an assistant which option fits.

He frames modern optimisation as a stack of six interconnected disciplines:

- **SEO** (Search Engine Optimisation) — be found. Crawled, indexed, ranked, clicked. Still the foundation; AI search retrieves before it synthesises.
- **SMO** (Social Media Optimisation) — be discovered and discussed. A parallel discovery and authority layer; what the wider web says about you, not what you say about yourself.
- **AEO** (Answer Engine Optimisation) — be the answer. Clear, extractable, question-aligned passages; not just FAQ schema.
- **GEO** (Generative Engine Optimisation) — be understood, mentioned and cited. Discovered → understood → retrieved → corroborated → cited. Entity consistency, topical authority, original data, third-party mentions, digital PR, fresh structured content.
- **DEO** (Decision Engine Optimisation) — be evaluated, recommended and chosen. Emerging and not yet standardised. When a user gives an assistant constraints ("accounting platform, 20-person UK firm, Xero, payroll, under £300, good support"), the AI has to discover candidates, understand attributes, compare, verify constraints, evaluate evidence and recommend. What it needs: suitability, price, availability, features, reputation, policies, location, comparability, trust. Success moves from "ChatGPT mentioned us" to "ChatGPT recommended us because we best satisfied the requirements". Share of AI decisions, not share of search.
- **SXO** (Search Experience Optimisation) — convert that visibility. UX, speed, trust signals, calls to action, conversion rate, checkout friction, lead generation, retention.

Under all six sits one foundation: entities + content + data + authority + trust. He argues against six separate strategies; build the shared foundation and understand how it feeds each layer.

His funnel: rankings → answers → citations → recommendations → selections → transactions. The modern journey: need → discovery → search/social/AI → answer → research and synthesis → comparison → AI recommendation → human or agent decision → experience → conversion.

Four screenshots close the piece, running the same query on Google AI Mode (PromoSEO cited, FatRank chosen), ChatGPT (only FatRank recommended), Gemini (PromoSEO cited but not listed) and Claude (FatRank recommended, PromoSEO absent). Same two brands, four different outcomes.

Closing line: being visible is valuable; being chosen is better.

## Where it agrees with our thesis

The whole argument is the funnel moving down, which is our "it's not about position 8". His DEO layer — being decision-ready with price, suitability, availability, location, policies, corroboration and comparability — is our business record and the agent-facing endpoint described by someone else. His foundation is what the record and the corroboration engine exist to produce. His four-engine screenshot is the cleanest public illustration of why source weights and prompt outcomes have to be tracked per engine rather than assumed.

## Where he stops and we go further

His stack is a taxonomy of disciplines: six acronyms an agency can sell as six services. Ours is a single question: does this asset make money.

He ends the funnel at "transactions" but never wires it. There is no measurement of what a citation, a recommendation or a landing page returned, no attribution to enquiries or revenue, and no fixing loop. SXO sits at the end of his list as a separate discipline; in our model conversion is the same chain as visibility, on the same asset, in the same P&L.

He describes what to optimise. We are building the thing that tells a business which optimisations paid back, and does the ones that will.

## What to take from it

**Vocabulary.** "Decision-ready" is a better phrase than anything we've used for the record and the endpoint. "Share of AI decisions" is a metric that belongs on the front page next to enquiries.

**A sales frame.** Show a client the six layers, then say: we don't sell you six things, we tell you which ones are worth paying for. He has drawn the diagram we'd need to explain why the asset P&L matters.

**Evidence of the gap.** This is a UK lead-gen operator who guarantees ROI, and even he is writing about layers of optimisation rather than returns per asset. The gap is stated by the person you'd most expect to have closed it.

## Actions

- Adopt "decision-ready" and "share of AI decisions" in the vision (v1.5) and the pitch language.
- Use the six-layer stack as the opening frame in the proposal document; position the asset P&L as the thing that sits across all six.
- Add per-engine "cited vs chosen" as two distinct states in the monitoring data, since his screenshots show they diverge.
- Watch Dooley's channels (PromoSEO, FatRank, his podcasts) as a UK barometer for how the outcomes conversation moves.
